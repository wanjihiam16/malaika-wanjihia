About Me

Engineer. Researcher. Kenyan.

I'm a PhD student in Human-Centered Computing at UMBC and an Electrical Engineer at the Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory. Originally from Kenya, I'm driven by one question: how can AI systems truly serve everyone, especially those with the most to gain and the most to lose?

My research lives at the intersection of technology, trust, and human dignity, examining how vulnerable populations calibrate trust in AI, and how data workers who power humanoid robotic systems experience privacy and physical impact along the way.

Outside the lab, I run a hat business, jump out of planes, chase new food experiences around the world, and mentor the next generation of engineers and researchers.

Research

Understanding People in the Age of AI

My work examines the human side of artificial intelligence: how people develop trust, maintain agency, and experience the costs of the systems that shape their lives.

Current Project · UMBC

Privacy and Physical Impacts of Humanoid Robotic Data Work

The development of humanoid robots depends on human data workers who annotate, label, and generate training data, often in conditions that carry real privacy and physical costs. This project examines those experiences and what they reveal about the hidden human labor behind AI systems.
